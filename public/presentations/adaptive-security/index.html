01 · Core Workflows

Launching a campaign
shouldn't feel risky

Why the bet existed

80%

of people were already editing campaigns after they launched. Admins spotted something off and patched it live.

45%

of orgs that launched a training campaign created an explicitly test-named one first, just to see what employees would get.

What I walked into

Three reasons they launched scared

A wizard that hides the whole picture

Settings were spread across steps, so admins never saw the campaign as one thing before it went live.

No way to preview the inbox

They could preview a module, not the email. So they built fake campaigns to find out.

One toggle, two jobs

Compliance quietly controlled whether results posted to tools like Drata or Vanta, and that setting stayed hidden until the toggle was on.

The split

I built the one-pager. The team kept the wizard.

Prototype A dropped admins onto an empty campaign page. Every setting visible and editable in place. I built it in code so people could click a real flow.

Prototype B kept the stepper and fixed its edges. Both tested well. The synthesis was blunt: there isn't a definitely better approach. The team kept the lower-risk change to a workflow customers already knew.

A CISO, in concept testing

Participants kept inventing workarounds to see what employees would actually receive. Every throwaway campaign polluted the org's reporting. Neither prototype had solved this. It became the next brief.

Test Send

Send the real email, to yourself, before anyone else sees it

I wrote the brief and spec for speed, on a foundation that would last. The test uses the campaign's due dates. Each recipient gets it in their own language. I documented every notification macro so engineering and support worked from one list.

I wanted a single recipient picker for training and phishing. Engineering pushed back on the migration cost, so we shipped two pickers and logged the consolidation. That was right for speed.

Compliance

41.3% had the toggle on. 5.4% had a postback.

The compliance toggle also sent results to the GRC tool. One CISO only found that out from our prototype. Engineering confirmed the link was a frontend-only restriction, so the two could be pulled apart.

I split them. The tag became "Mark as Compliance for reporting." Postbacks got their own card. I designed it, wrote both PRs, merged them, and wrote the launch post.

Was it a success

2,081

test sends in the first 60 days. 1,487 training, 594 phishing, from at least 33 and 25 companies. Weekly training tests went from 93 to 295 in five weeks.

5

customer conversations about setting up compliance or postbacks, down from 12 in the same 60-day window. Setup tickets went from 4 to 1.

None of this is a measured outcome I want to dress up. Nobody has re-measured the 45% test-named baseline. Delivery issues rose from 7 to 11, because a label cannot fix an integration. Threads asking to see the real inbox held flat, but support now answers them by pointing to Test Send.

Biggest learning

A losing prototype
can still win

I treated Prototype A like something I needed to ship. Looking back, its job was to make people say what they needed out loud. "Send me a test" and "what does this toggle do" only came up because they were clicking through something real.

02 · Level up AICC

Training people
don't want to skip

The AI content creator

The pitch is speed.
The catch is control.

Admins generate a module from a prompt instead of picking from a library. Then they have to make it theirs. Fix a frame, add a quiz, match the brand. Without a design background.

My scope was interactive blocks, the editing model in the frame builder, layouts, and narration. The biggest open question on the bet was how much the AI should drive.

What the model was good at

If it was all interactive, I think we'd turn off a number of users

Customer call, security admin

The model was great at theming a module to a customer's brand. Interactivity was the trap. People were clear that training is not a game. The takeaway we carried forward: brand freely, interact sparingly.

How much should AI drive

I assumed freeform would win. Hybrid did.

I built a coded prototype of an agent-first frame builder and tested three editing modes with five security and training teams. AI-only lost every time. People liked what the AI made. They wanted the last word.

A fully manual canvas brings back the blank-page problem AICC exists to solve. Hybrid was the clear winner. AI for the draft, small direct controls for the fixes.

AI-only

Every change goes through a prompt. Lost every session.

Hybrid

AI does the heavy lifting. A side panel for small manual tweaks.

Full freeform

A manual canvas where admins can move anything. Brings the blank page back.

add block doesn't mean anything

Concept test, session synthesis

Finding "Add block" was the main friction in 7 of 9 sessions. People wanted the last word and could not find the control that gave it to them. Moving the entry to the top made it easier to find. I folded that, per-frame revert, and the naming feedback into a synthesis with the lead designer and PM.

While the direction was still open

I shipped the pieces I could, in code

Was it a success

1,105

guided sessions in the first 14 days after launch was announced, against 141 in the 18 days before, when the flow was live but quiet.

95%

of people who submit a prompt still start an outline. Extra questions did not make them quit.

About 85% start creating a module and 77% see a finished one. Early feedback asked to keep prompting after a module was built. Nothing measures yet whether the output is less generic, which was the brief's real target. The quiz work was still open when I left.

Biggest learning

Control is a feature

I treated the editing question like a slider from all AI to all manual, and assumed the answer sat at one end. Customers kept telling us it isn't a slider. They want the AI to do the boring 80% and their own hands on the last 20%.

Designing for AI turned out to be mostly about designing the handoff back to the person, and making the thing they reach for impossible to miss.

What I take with me

A bold prototype is for what it makes people tell you
