Shopify Payments · Jan to Mar 2025

Why even fix payouts

Checkout was clear. Getting paid was not. Dual processors were about to double the mess, so we needed one merchant-facing model for where money comes from, when it arrives, and what happens next.

B2B fintech Legacy redesign

The gap

Merchants could sell with confidence and still not know when they would get paid

With Stripe and PayPal both in the mix, payout speed, status words, and multi-currency cases made the money feel random. Product, engineering, and design aligned on consolidating statuses and timings in the frontend.

You collected my money. You sent the money. You are the one who wrote the word "deposited".

Merchant support thread

I only see one day ahead, but i need to know more days, because i need to manage my cashflow.

Merchant support thread

Double trouble

The processors did not speak the same language. The admin had to.

Stripe marks a payout in transit once funds are sent. PayPal labels it delivered even if the bank takes another 3 to 5 days. We mapped the underlying API states to one merchant-facing model, so "deposited" meant the same thing no matter who moved the money.

The big 5

Varied totals

Payout amounts did not match across Admin home, Finance home, and the Payouts index.

Bank statement mismatch

A day's payouts could split across processors, so earnings and the bank line did not line up.

Confusing statuses

"Scheduled" showed as "Pending" on Finances. The same badge on an order meant something else: waiting to be batched.

Payout timing differences

In transit and delivered did not mean the money was in the account.

Unclear cash flow

The page only showed the next scheduled payout. Daily merchants could see one day ahead.

Payouts index

They came for the next payout, not a graph

"To be paid" looked like the next deposit. It was a larger total. I switched the hero number to next payout, put money movement on a timeline, and hinted at the destination bank so the mental model had an endpoint.

A wildcard with product photos and a big chart looked interesting and failed the job. Merchants come here for concrete numbers.

Payout details

The timeline had to shrink so the money could stay in view

First pass put a predicted deposit date on the timeline and overemphasized it. Fees sat in a dropdown that collided with the word "charges." The final details page keeps the date, drops the extra clicks when there is only one fee, and uses a compact timeline with icons for movement.

Biggest learning

I almost let the edge cases write the page

At first I optimized for every exception. Multi-entity and multi-currency shops are about 10%. Reserve-based payouts for fraud add more. Designing around them would have made the core worse. The system had to scale to those cases without letting them dictate the primary design.
